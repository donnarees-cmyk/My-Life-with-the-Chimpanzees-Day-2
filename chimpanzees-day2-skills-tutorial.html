    🔍 Cite evidence

    Standard: ELA.K12.EE.1.1 · Cite evidence to explain and justify reasoning

    Don’t just say it. Prove it!

    A claim is what you think. Evidence is the proof from the text. Reasoning explains how the proof backs up your claim.

    💪 Claim: The author is patient.
    🔍 Evidence: The text says she sat still on the bank from sunrise until noon.
    💡 Reasoning: This shows she was willing to wait quietly for hours, and that is what patience means.
    • 📝 Paraphrase: say the evidence in your own words.
    • ❝ Short quote: copy a few exact words and put them in quotation marks.
    • 🗣️ Starters: “According to the text…”, “The author says…”, “This shows…”

    ✅ Strong evidence directly proves your claim. Evidence that is just a random detail does not.

    Find the proof

    Practice passage written for this tutorial (not from the selection) The first week at the river camp, the otters ignored me. I sat on the muddy bank from sunrise until noon, keeping very still, and wrote down everything: how they dove, how they rolled, how they called to each other with high, squeaky whistles. On the eighth day, a young otter surfaced near my boots and sniffed the air. It stared at me for a full minute before it slid back into the water. My legs were numb and my notebook was soaked, but I was grinning. The otters had decided I was not a threat.

    📚 Read complex texts

    Standard: ELA.K12.EE.2.1 · Read and comprehend grade-level complex texts

    Tough text? You have a toolbox!

    Hard texts have long sentences, big words, and lots of ideas. Good readers pause and check: “Does this make sense?” If not, they use a tool.

    ✂️ Chunk itBreak a long sentence at commas and dashes. Read one piece at a time.
    🔁 RereadGo back and read it slowly again.
    🧩 Context cluesUse nearby words to figure out a new word.
    ❓ Ask questions“Who? What? Why?”
    📝 SummarizeSay the main idea in your own words after each paragraph.
    🎨 Picture itMake a movie in your mind.

    Use your tools

    Practice passage written for this tutorial (not from the selection) Primatologists—scientists who study monkeys and apes—must often endure conditions that would discourage most people. Humidity thickens the air until clothing clings like a second skin; leeches drop from leaves; downpours erase trails within minutes. Yet the observer’s task is paradoxical: to learn how animals behave naturally, the researcher must become, in effect, unnoticeable. Any sudden movement can alter the very behavior she hopes to record.

    💡 Make inferences

    Standard: ELA.K12.EE.3.1 · Make inferences to support comprehension

    Read between the lines

    An inference is a smart conclusion about something the author does not say directly. It is not a wild guess. You build it from clues.

    🔎 Text clues
    what the author wrote
    +
    🧠 What I know
    from real life
    =
    💡 Inference
    what it probably means

    Example: “Sam checked the sky, packed an umbrella, and put on his boots.” Clues: umbrella and boots. I know: people use those when it rains. Inference: Sam thinks it will rain.

    🗣️ Starter: “I read that… and I know that… so I infer…”
